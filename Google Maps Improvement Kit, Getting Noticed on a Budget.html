How To Use This Kit

You don't need a marketing degree to show up on Google Maps — you need a short list of the right things, done in the right order. That's what this kit is.

Each section takes 15–30 minutes. Do Section 1 today. It's the highest-impact, lowest-effort fix in this entire kit, and most business owners have never done it properly.

  • 1.Profile Audit — Fix your Google Business Profile basics. Do this first, today.
  • 2.Trust Signals — A reference list of easy, legitimate places to get your shop listed elsewhere.
  • 3.Review Snowball — A simple system (with a script) to get more Google reviews, consistently.
  • 4.Weekly Tracker — A 20-minute weekly habit so visibility doesn't slide backward.
  • 5.Ranking Log — A monthly log to prove — in your own numbers — that it's working.

Score Your Listing

Before you dive in, find out where you actually stand. Answer honestly, not aspirationally — your score tells you how urgently you need Section 1.

Count your checks:
0–3: Start today — you're invisible to most searches right now. Do Section 1 this afternoon.
4–7: You have a foundation, but you're leaking customers to businesses that finished the checklist. Sections 1–3 are your priority.
8–10: You're ahead of most local businesses. Focus on Sections 4–5 to protect that lead.
01

The 15-Minute Profile Audit

Fix the basics most shops get wrong — do this section today.

Why this matters

Google decides who shows up on the map using three things: relevance, distance, and prominence. An incomplete or inaccurate profile scores lower on relevance and prominence — no matter how good your shop actually is — and a customer who hits wrong hours simply goes to the next name on the map instead of coming back later.

Open your listing here: business.google.com

Quick win: Items 1, 3, and 6 alone fix the majority of "why can't customers find us" complaints. If you only have 15 minutes today, start there.

02

The Local Trust Signals List

Legitimate, low-effort places to get your shop listed elsewhere.

Google trusts a business more when it's mentioned consistently across other real, local sources — not through paid link schemes, just the ordinary places a shop like yours should already be. Work through this list once, then revisit twice a year.

Skip anything that asks you to pay for a "guaranteed backlink" or "ranking package." Those don't help and can violate Google's guidelines.
