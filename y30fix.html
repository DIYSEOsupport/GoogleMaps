The 15-Minute Profile Audit

Fix the basics most shops get wrong — do this section today.

Why this matters

Google decides who shows up on the map using three things: relevance, distance, and prominence. An incomplete or inaccurate profile scores lower on relevance and prominence — no matter how good your shop actually is — and a customer who hits wrong hours simply goes to the next name on the map instead of coming back later.

02

The Local Trust Signals List

Legitimate, low-effort places to get your shop listed elsewhere.

Why this matters

Google cross-checks your business info against how it's listed elsewhere on the web — this is called a "citation." The more consistently your name, address, and phone number show up across legitimate sources, the more Google trusts that you're a real, established business, which directly affects how high you rank.

Work through this list once, then revisit twice a year.

Skip anything that asks you to pay for a "guaranteed backlink" or "ranking package." Those don't help and can violate Google's guidelines.

How to get a QR code for your review link

Paste your Google review link into whichever of these your business already uses — no design skills needed:

Chrome (desktop or mobile) — open the review link, then right-click the page (or tap the Share icon in the address bar) and choose Create QR Code, then Download.
Microsoft Edge — right-click the page and choose Create QR code for this page, then Download.
Safari or Firefox — these don't generate QR codes natively. Use a free tool like qrcode-monkey.com, paste your review link in, and download the image — no signup required.

Print the code on receipts, a small counter card, or a window cling — anywhere a customer has 10 idle seconds.

Handling a Negative Review

One bad review, handled well and in public, often builds more trust than five quiet good ones — future customers read your response, not just the complaint.

Quick Glossary

A few terms that show up in local SEO advice, translated into plain English.

Google Business Profile (GBP) — Your free business listing that shows up on Google Maps and in search results. This used to be called "Google My Business."
NAP — Name, Address, Phone number — shorthand for the three details that need to match exactly everywhere your business is listed.
Citation — Any mention of your business's NAP on another website or directory (Yelp, Bing, Chamber of Commerce, etc.). Consistent citations build trust with Google.
Local pack / map pack — The group of 3 business listings with a map that shows up at the top of a local Google search — the most valuable real estate in local search.
Prominence — One of Google's three local ranking factors — roughly, how well-known and well-reviewed your business is, both online and off.
Primary category — The single main category Google uses to decide which searches your listing shows up for. The most important setting in your whole profile.
